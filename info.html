Methods and definitions

How it works

Every measure in the app, explained in plain language—including its formula, evidence requirements, and limitations.

Transparency principle

These measures describe patterns in the imported log. They do not diagnose recovery, prescribe a universal training target, or know your goals unless the data itself establishes something.

Foundations

Logged set

Each imported Strong set is treated as one hard set. The app assumes it was performed close enough to failure to count.

Volume = number of logged sets

The Insights headline “Total volume” is this whole-program logged-set count. Muscle-group total volume is the separate weighted measure explained below.

Weight × reps, sometimes called tonnage, is deliberately not used as the volume measure.

Training session

A session is a unique combination of the Strong workout identifier and date. Multiple exercises and sets in one workout still count as one session.

Session key = date + workout number

Weeks and selected periods

Weekly charts group data Monday through Sunday. Headline totals use the exact selected dates; weekly scores use only complete Monday–Sunday weeks so every compared week contains seven days.

Partial edge weeks are excluded from Consistency, Opportunity, Efficiency, Recovery proxy, and plateau calculations on Insights.

Exercise-to-muscle mapping

exercise-muscles.js assigns primary and secondary muscles to each exercise. Unmapped exercises remain in workout totals but are excluded from muscle-group calculations.

Exposure measures

Volume and frequency

Direct muscle volume

One logged set adds one direct set to every primary muscle mapped to that exercise.

Example: one barbell bench-press set adds one direct chest set.

Indirect muscle volume

One logged set adds one indirect set to every secondary muscle. Direct and indirect counts remain separate so supporting work is visible without being treated as primary work.

Weighted total volume

The body map and total-volume muscle views combine direct and indirect work using a half-set contribution for secondary muscles.

Weighted total = direct sets + (0.5 × indirect sets)

Average sets per week

The total sets inside the selected date range are divided by the exact number of calendar weeks represented by that range.

Average/week = sets ÷ (selected days ÷ 7)

Muscle frequency on Volume

A muscle is counted at most once per workout when it received any mapped direct or indirect set. The period figure is unique exposed sessions divided by weeks.

Frequency = exposed workout sessions ÷ weeks

Direct frequency in Opportunity

Opportunity is stricter: it counts only sessions containing at least one exercise for which the muscle is primary. Indirect-only exposure does not reduce the frequency-gap component.

Deload heuristic

A completed Monday–Sunday week qualifies when it contains training, but its direct mapped set count is at most 50% of the preceding four completed weeks’ average.

This is a log pattern—not proof that the week was planned as a deload or that recovery was needed.

Load and reps

Performance measures

Estimated one-repetition maximum (e1RM)

The app uses the Epley formula for sets with a positive weight and rep count. It estimates—not measures—the maximum load for one repetition.

e1RM = weight × (1 + reps ÷ 30)

The weekly trend uses the highest estimate recorded for that exercise in each week. Different rep ranges can produce differently reliable estimates.

Comparable observations

Performance comparisons use the same exercise and require median rep counts to differ by no more than reps.

This reduces misleading comparisons between very different rep-range phases.

Notable estimated-1RM PR

The best e1RM inside the selected period must exceed the best estimate logged before that period for the same exercise.

Percentage increase = (new best − previous best) ÷ previous best × 100

PRs are sorted by percentage increase.

Lift frequency

Lift workouts count unique sessions containing the selected exercise. Weeks trained count Monday–Sunday weeks containing at least one such session.

The median e1RM of the most recent three comparable sessions is compared with the preceding three. A possible plateau requires the change to stay within ±%.

Coach page

Coaching review logic

Review period

The Coach uses the latest imported workout as the endpoint and reviews up to rolling weeks. Days after the latest import are never treated as missed training.

Sustained regularity

A positive consistency signal needs at least covered weeks, activity in at least % of them, and no substantial recent frequency reduction.

Progression signal

Requires at least exercise sessions over weeks. It recognizes sustained e1RM improvement of at least %, one more rep at similar load, or at least % more load at comparable reps.

Coach possible plateau

The recent-three versus preceding-three median e1RM change must be within ±%, without meaningful load or rep progress. It is always phrased as possible, never confirmed.

Frequency and volume changes

The Coach compares two complete -week blocks. Changes of at least % in session frequency or % in direct volume can trigger review language.

Boundaries

What the data cannot tell us

Effort: every logged set is assumed to count, but missing RPE means proximity to failure is unknown.

Recovery: performance stability cannot establish fatigue, sleep quality, stress, nutrition, soreness, injury, or readiness.

Goals and schedule: the app does not infer a target muscle, intended training frequency, or planned routine from past averages.

Causation: simultaneous changes in volume and performance are correlations in the log; they do not prove one caused the other.

Exercise changes: estimates are never compared across different exercise names, and a new variation may have too little history to score.

Missing data: unmapped exercises and incomplete weeks reduce coverage and are reported instead of silently filled in.
